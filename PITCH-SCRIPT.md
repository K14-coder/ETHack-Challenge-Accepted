## Read this first: two things on the slides

**1. Slide 4 shows eight specifications, but the arithmetic needs nine.**
The boxes multiply to 5,832, not 17,496. The missing one is the **evidence bar**,
which has 3 options. The script below says *"nine choices"* and never counts the
boxes out loud, so this passes unless someone multiplies. If a judge does the
arithmetic in Q&A, the answer is: *"Nine axes. The evidence bar, how much of the
peer group must report a field before it counts, is the ninth and it is on the
appendix slide."* It is on slide 11, so you can turn to it.

**2. Slide 12 says "equal wieghts".** Appendix slide, judges read appendices. Two
seconds to fix if you still can.

# SLIDE 2 · Have you ever measured a dog? · 10 sec · 26 words

> "Have you ever measured a dog?
>
> It sounds trivial. It is not. How tall is a dog? To the shoulder, or to the
> top of the head? How long is it, with the tail or without?"

**Delivery.** Ask the first line like a real question and pause half a second.
Look at the room, not the screen. Do not smile at your own joke; let them.

---

# SLIDE 3 · Let us measure a dog · 18 sec · 45 words

*Video plays, 8 seconds. Say nothing over it. Let it run.*

Then:

> "Same dog. Same tape measure. Four different numbers, and every single one of
> them is correct.
>
> Now replace the dog with a company, and the tape measure with an ESG rating.
> The agency publishes one number. They never tell you which measurement they
> took."

**Delivery.** The pause while the video runs is the most valuable silence in the
pitch. Do not fill it. Start speaking the moment it ends.

# SLIDE 4 · Why the industry disagrees · 32 sec · 80 words

> "And this is not a small effect. Berg, Kölbel and Rigobon took the
> disagreement between the major raters apart.
>
> *(point left)* **Fifty-six percent is measurement**, the same thing measured
> differently. **Thirty-eight percent is scope**, what counts at all. And **six
> percent is the weighting** — which is the only part any provider ever lets you
> change.
>
> *(point right)* So we went after the other ninety-four. **Nine choices**,
> every one of them a fork where MSCI and Bloomberg publish something different.
> Every combination of them.
>
> **Seventeen thousand four hundred and ninety-six ways to score the same
> company.**"

**Delivery.**
- Land 56 / 38 / 6 slowly. Everything else can move.
- Half-second pause after **"six percent"**. That number is why the project exists.
- Say **"nine choices"**, not "eight". Do not point at individual boxes and count.
- The last line is the punchline of the slide. Slow down and let the number sit.

# SLIDE 5 · A curve, not a number · 18 sec · 45 words

> "So a company does not have a score. It has a curve.
>
> ExxonMobil is second under some of those methods and fourteenth under others.
> Eighth most often. Nobody publishes that shape. They publish the tick mark in
> the middle and throw the rest away.
>
> The width of that curve is the number we think is missing."

**Delivery.** Trace the shape of the curve on the screen with your hand as you
say "second under some, fourteenth under others". Physical gesture, it reads
from the back.

# SLIDE 7 · Why the energy sector · 14 sec · 38 words

> "Why energy? Because raters disagree here more than in any other sector.
> **0.55** agreement, against 0.77 in tech.
>
> And does our measure behave? *(point right)* It should show more doubt where
> the data is thinner. It does. Across seventy-two company-pillar pairs,
> **Spearman minus 0.35**."

**Then switch to the browser. Do not linger.**

**If challenged on the 0.55 later:** it is Lopez 2020, reached through Nasdaq's
2024 ESG ratings review. Say it is a secondary citation and that you have not
read Lopez directly.

**Never quote a correlation across the four pillars.** That is n = 4 and p = 0.17.
The 72-pair number is the one that holds.

# LIVE DEMO · 50 sec · four clicks, nothing else

Every number below is what the screen will actually show. Verified.

### Click 0 · orient · 7 sec

*Point at the table. Do not click.*

> "Eighteen energy companies, ranked. Consensus here. And this bar is how much
> of that rank is a choice rather than a fact."

### Click 1 · the weight slider · 12 sec

*Drag **Environment** up to about 70%.*

> "This is the part every provider gives you. The weighting. Watch the settled
> counter. *(pause)* **It does not move. Still two.**
>
> That is the six percent, live."

**Screen:** 17,496 specifications · settled stays **2** · a couple of rows swap.
**Nothing dramatic happens, and that is the point. Do not promise a big move.**

### Click 2 · the question · 13 sec

*Hit **reset all**, then pin **Direction → level**.*

> "Now the part nobody gives you. This is the question itself. Cleanest today,
> or improving fastest? I pin it to today.
>
> **Two becomes thirty-three.**"

**Screen:** 8,748 specifications · settled **33**

### Click 3 · the evidence bar · 10 sec

*Pin **Evidence bar → strict**.*

> "And this is how demanding I am about evidence. **Thirty-three becomes
> fifty-six.** Fifty-six of a hundred and fifty-three comparisons hold. A
> published league table asserts all hundred and fifty-three."

**Screen:** 2,916 specifications · settled **56**

### Click 4 · the kill shot · 8 sec

*Flip **Direction → change**.*

> "Same data, same strictness, other question. **Fifty-six collapses to nine,
> and the entire podium changes.**"

**Screen:** settled **9** · top three flips from BKR, EXE, SLB to CTRA, OXY, WMB

**If the demo breaks:** *"The tool is in the repo and every number is in the
paper."* Go straight to slide 8. Never debug on stage.

# SLIDE 9 · $1bn allocation · 26 sec · 65 words

> "Tomorrow the world commits to net zero. So what actually changes?
>
> *(walk the chain left to right)* A **social agreement** becomes **legislation**.
> Legislation becomes **penalties and rewards**. And penalties and rewards become
> **financial impact**.
>
> Which means environmental performance stops being a reputational question and
> becomes a cash-flow question.
>
> In our framework that is not a new model. It is a slider. **Fifty percent
> environment, fifty percent financial resilience** — because under that
> scenario, they are the same bet."

**Delivery.** Move your hand along the chain as you say it. The whole argument is
that one causal sequence, so let the gesture carry it.

**If asked "which companies?"** — *"The framework gives you the ranking under
that weighting, and it tells you which of those comparisons are actually
settled. We would only act on the settled ones."*
