# Challenge brief

Challenge #1, ETHack 2026. Captured from the kick-off slides and talk, 12 Sep morning.

## Verbatim brief

> Build a data-driven framework to quantify and compare the sustainability of companies in the S&P 500.
>
> How you approach the problem is up to you. Define what sustainability means, identify and justify the most relevant indicators, source the necessary data, and develop a methodology to score, rank, or compare companies. Your solution may incorporate environmental, financial, operational, or other dimensions you consider relevant.
>
> **Bonus question**: Tomorrow, the world commits to reaching net-zero emissions as fast as possible. You manage a $1 billion investment fund. How do you allocate your portfolio under this new scenario, and why?
>
> We expect you to choose your own dataset, justify your choice, and show how it is suitable for the challenge.

## Sponsor and judges

Main sponsors: **Citadel** and **Citadel Securities**. Partners: ETH Zürich, Focus Water, ALBA, ETH Get Hired.

Judges: the organizer, James (ETH), Bianca (ETH), Maggie (MIT), Cheryl (Julius Bär, name heard as "Julius Pear" in the room, verify the spelling before naming her).

Read the room this implies: a quant trading firm plus two academics plus a private bank. They will not be impressed by a pretty dashboard with arbitrary weights. They will ask where the number came from and what it is sensitive to.

## What they actually want

A defensible method, not a product. Define sustainability explicitly, pick indicators you can defend one by one, get real data, score the S&P 500, and be honest about what the score cannot tell you. Then use the same machinery to answer the $1B allocation question, so the bonus is an output of the framework rather than a separate opinion.

## Judging criteria

Five criteria, each scored 0 to 5. No published weights, so treat them as equal: 25 points total.

Scale: 0 absent or not attempted · 1 barely there, mostly assertion · 2 attempted but weak or unclear · 3 solid, meets expectations · 4 strong, clearly above the field · 5 exceptional, best of the day.

| Criterion | Official wording | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| Impact | What impact could your proposed solution have on society, and who would benefit from it? | Name the actual user: an allocator, a regulator, a company. Not "society". |
| Innovation | Is the approach original or a fresh angle on a known problem? Would a team have arrived here without thinking hard? | ESG scoring is the most done-to-death problem in the room. The angle has to be the differentiator. |
| Technical Execution | How much actually works? Quality of the build relative to what two days allows. | They said they will read the GitHub repo. Clean, reproducible pipeline over notebook spaghetti. |
| Feasibility | Could this survive past the weekend? Realistic about cost, data, adoption, and constraints. | Free and licensable data sources. Say out loud what a paid feed would change. |
| Presentation | Clear problem framing, working demo, honest about limitations, finished on time. | 3 minutes, hard stop. "Honest about limitations" is in the rubric, so a stated weakness earns points. |
